RANIN · Pitch 2026

Two fronts

جبهتان

The empire of rights

The outstretched hand in the name of “women’s rights” often seeks approval more than change. Women become stories to tell, preferably painful ones, enough to move people and make them feel on the right side. But when the story turns into a concrete request, that hand withdraws. There’s a spritz waiting.

The guardians of order

Regimes backed by the West set themselves up as guardians of morality, while schools, hospitals and justice fall behind. Social frustration is steered toward those who challenge the rules, and women often end up in the crosshairs. In public, virtue is defended; in private, what is condemned is practiced. Morality matters most when it serves to control others.

We are not a generation number

لسنا رقمَ جيل

The first to arrive

A new language to learn, bureaucracy to face, qualifications that lose their value. She has to keep proving her abilities, often more than others, while trying to rebuild a network of relationships far from everything she knew.

Those born here

She speaks several languages and moves across cultures and generations. Family and society seem to ask for opposite things, but sometimes end up imposing the same one: never being enough.

What gets passed on (and what doesn’t)

She looks for her roots, rediscovers them, questions them and reinvents them. She grows up among references that don’t always match, expectations that contradict each other and spaces of freedom still to be won. She doesn’t want to choose between two worlds. She wants to live in both without constantly having to justify who she is.

What it’s about

عمّا يتحدّث

Identity

Like jasmine, the roots stay, but you can bloom elsewhere. Even when your own scent goes unrecognized.

Body

Soft as a fig, desired and judged. A body everyone has something to say about, except the one who lives in it.

Languages

Arabic and Italian mix like bread. Words contaminate each other, but sometimes they aren’t enough in either language.

Faith

Sweet as the date that breaks the fast. Nourishment, memory or tradition. It is shared, discussed, not imposed.

Inheritance

Like the olive tree, roots cross generations and borders. We inherit memories and traditions, but also silences and contradictions.

Ambitions

Like corn on the embers: it transforms, it burns, but it doesn’t go out. Not everyone starts with the same possibilities.

Pleasure

Enclosed, juicy, ready to burst. Female pleasure is multiple, intimate and not always willing to respect prohibitions.

Feminisms

Like mint: tear it out and it grows back stronger. It roots wherever it wants and ends up taking the whole garden.

Why Milan

لماذا ميلانو

And not another city

The city of those who arrive

A crossroads of stories and belongings.

Milan welcomes those who come from abroad, from the South and from the provinces. Some stay, some leave again, some try to build a life. A multicultural city where opportunity, inequality and new forms of community coexist.

The city of image

Where appearing matters almost as much as being.

Fashion, design, social media and the cult of image. In a city that celebrates individuality and emancipation, the female body is still subject to judgments, expectations and models to live up to.

The city of the night

By day we work, by night we reinvent ourselves.

Between university, offices, precarious jobs and family responsibilities, the night opens other spaces. Music, clubs, encounters and relationships become chances to experiment with freedoms that find less room during the day.

Who it’s for

لِمَن هو

01

The diaspora

Sees itself.

Those who live these things find themselves in it, and maybe feel a little less alone.

02

The outside gaze

Questions.

For those who think they already know what it means to be a woman. RANIN questions stereotypes and beliefs, showing a complexity that rarely finds space in the dominant narrative.

The goal: to get the complexity across. Not necessarily to resolve it.

Why Ranin

لماذا رنين

Not a film about the women of the diaspora.
A film by the women of the diaspora.

The gaze

Belonging is never just one.

Between colonial legacies, class differences and family memories, identities are built, contradict themselves and transform. Without having to choose just one part of oneself.

The form

Contradiction is life.

Across generations, languages and different ways of understanding freedom. Irony runs through the conflicts, habits and contradictions of women who are not trying to be a role model.

The impact

To exist without having to explain oneself.

To recognize oneself in complexity, without having to justify it. To question the hierarchies that decide which voices count, which stories deserve space and who gets to tell them.

The protagonists

البطلات

From the collective
Dardashe

01

Dardashe is a feminist collective of women of the diaspora, based in Milan and active across the rest of Italy. It organizes meetings, workshops, events and cultural and artistic initiatives.

02

The possible protagonists of RANIN are part of this community. Women of different ages, professions and paths, each with her own relationship to her origins, family and everyday life.

03

The documentary will follow some of them beyond the collective’s meetings, in the places and relationships that are part of their lives.

Three movements

ثلاث حركات

I

Life

Everyday gestures, passions, friendships, music and desires. The protagonists tell their stories through what they do, what they love and the way they inhabit the world.

II

Expectations

Family conventions, generational conflicts and class differences intertwine with racism, exoticization and the expectations of emancipation of Western society. Freedom is called into question by both sides.

III

The space we make

Differences become occasions for clashes, irony, complicity and solidarity. No one claims to represent everyone. Each builds her own space, opening it to those who come after.

Visual language

اللغة البصرية

How we shoot it

Light

The light of places, of people.

Natural light, shadows on faces, home interiors and streets. Without chasing an exotic or idealized aesthetic.

Camera

Close, without intruding.

The camera observes, takes part and leaves room. In everyday gestures, glances, conversations and silences. 

Color

The colors of everyday life.

Fabrics, walls, objects, plants and city lights. A living palette, between warm tones and pop colors, without reducing cultural identity to a recognizable aesthetic.

Yes

Lived-in homes, bodies in motion, everyday details, music, overlapping languages and spontaneous moments. Mess, irony and imperfection have room too.

No

Staged folklore, cultural symbols added as decoration, images of women reduced to victims or heroines. No pursuit of the exotic to satisfy the viewer’s gaze.

The author

المؤلفة

Sara
Emara

Italian-Egyptian, raised and trained in Cairo, part of the diaspora in Italy. A member of the feminist collective Dardashe.

Too Italian in Egypt, too Egyptian in Italy. And yet, in both places, I am at home.

In Egypt

The privilege of also being Italian.

I grew up here and I know the language, and I had to bend to the rules of society. A society with its own problems, in which I was nonetheless privileged: my Italian passport gave me a status that not everyone has. I belong to this place, but I don’t live under the same conditions.

In Italy

Belonging can’t be taken for granted.

I was born here and I speak the language, but I studied and grew up in Egypt. I don’t share many of the cultural references of those who grew up in Italy, and I had to fight to be taken seriously, to prove my abilities and to win a space.
